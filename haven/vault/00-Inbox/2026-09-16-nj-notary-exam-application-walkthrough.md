# The NJ notary exam and commissioning application — the walkthrough

What the sitting actually is, what it costs, and what happens after. Companion to
[[2026-09-16-given-word-notary-llc-filing-walkthrough]] — **the two are independent and both can
be done the same day.**

**Where it all happens:** `njportal.com/DOR/NotaryExam` — the state's own portal. Everything below
is one continuous flow on that site. The exam is **online and on-demand**: no seat to book, no
proctor, no scheduled sitting.

---

## THE ONE THING THAT DECIDES THE OUTCOME

**The exam comes out of the New Jersey Notary Public Manual.** Not general notary knowledge, not
the bootcamp's summary, not a practice-test app — the State's own manual, plus its official
training-video series. New non-attorney applicants are expected to complete a **six-hour course of
study**, and that is what the six hours are.

**Read the manual. Do not skim it and do not substitute a study guide for it.** The questions are
drawn from it directly, which cuts both ways: it is a closed, knowable body of material, so
reading it properly is close to a guarantee, and not reading it properly is close to the opposite.

---

## THE EXAM ITSELF

| | |
|---|---|
| Questions | **50** |
| To pass | **40 correct — 80%** |
| Time limit | **75 minutes** |
| Format | Online, taken through the state portal |
| Fee | **$2.50, and it covers three attempts** |
| If all three fail | Start a new commissioning application and pay again |

**75 minutes for 50 questions is 90 seconds each.** That is comfortable if the manual has been
read and impossible if it is being looked up mid-exam. Time is not the constraint here; preparation
is.

**Three attempts on one fee is real breathing room** — it means a first attempt is not a
catastrophe. It is not a reason to wing it, because burning all three means starting the whole
application over.

**Do not confuse this with the NNA signing agent exam.** That one is separate, private,
45 questions at 80%, and part of the $199+ certification package for loan signing work. This is the
State's commissioning exam, and it is the one that makes the commission legal. Passing the NNA exam
without this one means nothing; this one is the licence.

## THE STEPS, IN ORDER

### Step 1 — Check eligibility (2 minutes)

The standard NJ requirements: **18 or older**, **a New Jersey resident** (or a non-resident who
works or maintains an office in NJ), **able to read and write English**, and **no disqualifying
criminal conviction**. Confirm the current list in the manual rather than from any summary,
including this one.

### Step 2 — Do the six-hour course (the real work)

Read the **Notary Public Manual** end to end. Watch the **full training-video series**. This is the
prerequisite the exam is built on, and it is the only step that takes real time.

### Step 3 — Start a New Commissioning Application

The exam is **not a standalone page** — it lives inside a New Commissioning Application on the
portal. Starting that application is how the exam is reached.

### Step 4 — Take the exam ($2.50)

50 questions, 40 to pass, 75 minutes, three attempts included.

### Step 5 — Submit the application ($25 + $5 online convenience fee)

Passing unlocks the application itself. Fill it in and submit.

### Step 6 — Legislator endorsement (nobody controls this)

**The application is forwarded to a State Legislator for endorsement.** It is a normal step in the
NJ process, not a red flag or a favour to arrange, and its timing is out of anyone's hands. It is
part of why the total wait is measured in weeks.

### Step 7 — Commission forms arrive by mail (~2 weeks)

Roughly two weeks from submission, the Commission Packet arrives at the address on the application.

### Step 8 — Swear the oath at the county clerk, in person ($15) — **90 days, hard**

Take the Certificate and Oath Qualification to the **clerk of the county of residence** and swear
the oath. **Ninety days from appointment, and the Treasurer cancels the commission if it is
missed.** There is no version of this that happens by mail or online.

**Put the deadline in Haven with a `due` the day the packet arrives** so calendar-sync rings it.
Missing it means starting the entire process over, and it is exactly the kind of date that gets
lost in a busy month. This is already written into [[2026-09-16-notary-commission-lifecycle]].

### Step 9 — Get the seal, and the commission is live

Only after the oath. Store the seal locked and **separate from the journal** — the loss playbook
covers why.

## WHAT TO DO WHILE WAITING

The wait between step 5 and step 8 is **weeks, and it is the most valuable time in the whole plan**
because everything that does not need a commission can run inside it:

- **File the LLC and get the EIN** — see the LLC walkthrough. Completely independent of this.
- **Google Business Profile**, submitted for verification, not published. The longest queue there is.
- **Signing agent certification study and the background screening** — another queue, started early.
- **E&O insurance** — check whether the carrier needs a commission number first.
- **Open the BlueNotary account and run the six-field trial**, before any real journal entry exists.
- **Send DORES the four open questions.** They are slow, so asking now means answers arrive about
  when they are needed.

**Do not publish the booking form or the Business Profile.** Taking a booking for an act that
cannot yet legally be performed is worse than having no front end at all.
