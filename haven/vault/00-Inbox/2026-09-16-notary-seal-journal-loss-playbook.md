# Playbook — notary seal or journal lost, stolen, or damaged

Read this on the day it happens. It is written to be followed while rattled, in a parking lot,
on a phone, which is exactly when it will be needed.

**The rule that matters most, before anything else: stop notarizing.** From the moment the seal
is out of Lemar's possession until a replacement is in hand, every notarization stops. Using a
seal already reported missing, or one that may be in someone else's hands, creates real
liability and is the thing that turns an inconvenience into a commission problem.

Related: [[2026-09-15-notary-business-backend-systems]] (the project brief and locked plan).

## The six steps, in order

1. **Stop.** No further notarial acts, in person or remote. Cancel or reschedule anything on
   the calendar today. Samira can move appointments; she cannot decide the commission is safe
   to use.
2. **Search properly once.** Car, bag, last appointment location, home office. One thorough
   pass, not a day of hoping — the reporting clock is the thing that matters and hoping burns
   it.
3. **Report to the commissioning authority.** The NJ Division of Revenue and Enterprise
   Services, through the notary portal or in writing. Standard practice is a signed letter
   carrying his full name, the commission number, the commission expiry, and **the last date
   the seal or journal was known to be in his possession**. Keep a copy of what was sent and
   the date it went.
4. **Report to police if theft is suspected**, and keep the report number. A stolen seal is a
   fraud instrument in someone else's hands, and the report is what separates a victim from a
   participant if it is later misused.
5. **Order the replacement**, and only resume notarizing once it physically arrives.
6. **Write it up.** A Haven note recording what was lost, when it was last held, what was
   reported, to whom, on what date, and when normal service resumed. This is the record that
   answers the question years later.

## If it is the journal rather than the seal

The same reporting duty applies, and the stakes are different in kind. The journal carries
signer names, addresses and identification details under a ten-year retention duty, so a lost
journal is potentially a **data breach** as well as a compliance event. Two additions:

- Consider whether NJ's breach-notification obligations are triggered by what the journal
  contained.
- The monthly export in the `Notary — Journal Exports` Drive folder is what makes the
  retention duty survivable. **It is the reason that export exists.** Losing the journal
  without it means losing the record entirely; losing it with the export means losing at most
  one month.

That is also the argument for never letting the export slip: it is dull insurance right up
until the day it is the only copy.

## What Samira may and may not do

**May:** reschedule or cancel affected appointments, draft the report letter in Lemar's voice
for him to send, write the incident note, and set a reminder to confirm the replacement
arrived.

**May not:** decide the commission is safe to use again, send the report to DORES on his
behalf, or resume normal notary automations. **The alarm at two hours is suspended for the
duration** — there is no journal entry coming, because there are no acts being performed, and
an alarm firing every two hours through an incident is noise on the worst possible day.

## Prevention, which is cheaper than any of the above

- Seal stored locked and separate from the journal, so one loss is never both.
- Never left in a vehicle.
- Spare journal on hand, so a damaged one does not stop work.
- The monthly export never skipped.

## Verification status

The six-step shape, the stop-notarizing rule, and the report-to-authority-and-police steps are
**standard practice across US notary jurisdictions** and are what the profession's guidance
uniformly recommends. **NJ's specific reporting mechanics were not verified** — the exact form,
office, deadline, and whether a replacement requires a new commission number. Confirm with
DORES and record the answer here before this playbook is ever relied on. Until then, treat the
sequence as sound and the NJ paperwork detail as unconfirmed.
