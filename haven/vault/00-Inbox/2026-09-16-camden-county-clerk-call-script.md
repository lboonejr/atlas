# Call script — Camden County Clerk, trade name availability

One phone call, five minutes, and it unblocks the entire front end of the notary business.
Related: [[2026-09-15-notary-business-backend-systems]],
[[2026-09-20-notary-exam-day-checkpoint]].

**Numbers:** Camden Courthouse **(856) 225-5300** · County Store **(856) 566-2920**
No hours are posted anywhere public, so this call also establishes when the office is open.

## What the call is actually for

Two things, and it is worth being clear which is which:

1. **Confirm "Given Word Notary" is not already registered** as a trade name in Camden County.
   No online search tool exists, so the phone is the only route.
2. **Find out exactly what walking in with a completed filing requires** — so the trip happens
   once, not twice.

The second half matters more than it looks. The certificate must be notarized and every
registrant must appear in person, which means a wasted trip costs a whole day.

## The opening

> "Hi — I'm looking to file a Trade Name Certificate for a sole proprietorship. Before I come in,
> I wanted to check whether the name is already taken and make sure I bring everything I need."

Plain, specific, and it signals a prepared caller rather than a browser. Most clerks respond well
to someone who already knows what form they want.

## The four asks, in order

**1. Availability.**
> "The name is Given Word Notary — G-I-V-E-N, new word, W-O-R-D, then Notary. Is that already on
> file in Camden County?"

Spell it. "Given Word" sounds like "given word" in ordinary speech and will be misheard.

**If they will not search over the phone** — plenty of counties will not — do not push. Ask
instead:
> "Understood. Is there a way to check before I file, or do I find out at the counter?"

That answer is itself useful. If availability is only determined at the counter, the trip
absorbs the risk, and the fallback is simply to arrive prepared for a name conflict rather than
to keep calling.

**2. The in-person requirements.**
> "What do I need to bring? Is the form notarized before I arrive, or do you notarize it there?"

**Ask this one directly — it is the highest-value question in the call.** Many NJ county clerks
notarize on site, often free or for a nominal fee. If Camden does, the whole "find another notary
first" step disappears. If they do not, that becomes a separate errand to line up beforehand.

Also confirm: photo ID required, and whether every registrant must appear (expected yes, but
confirm, since it is a sole proprietorship with one registrant).

**3. Payment and fee.**
> "I have the fee at $57 — is that current? And what do you take, card or check?"

County offices are notoriously cash-or-check. Turning up with only a card is a wasted trip.

**4. Hours and whether an appointment is needed.**
> "What are your hours, and is this walk-in or do I need an appointment?"

## The one extra question worth asking

> "I'll be working across county lines — Gloucester, Burlington. Does this filing cover that, or
> do I file separately in each county?"

**A NJ trade name registration is county-specific and only effective in the county where it is
filed.** For a mobile notary who drives, that is a real question and not an academic one. The
clerk's answer settles whether the launch needs one $57 filing or three.

Do not decide anything on this during the call. Write the answer down and it gets weighed after.

## Write down, during the call

- Whether the name is available — and if they would not say, that fact
- Whether the office notarizes on site, and what it costs
- Current fee, and accepted payment methods
- Hours, and walk-in versus appointment
- The multi-county answer
- **Who said it and when.** A name and a date, because a second call that contradicts the first is
  common and the earlier answer is worth being able to cite.

## Two things not to do

**Do not ask the clerk for legal advice.** Whether to file a trade name or form an LLC is not the
clerk's question to answer, and an offhand counter opinion is not worth acting on. That decision
is already recorded in the brief — trade name now, LLC when signing-agent work starts.

**Do not file anything during the call.** This call is reconnaissance. Nothing gets paid, signed
or submitted over the phone.

## After the call

Append the answers here as an Update, then the trade name filing is a scheduled errand rather
than an open question — and the Google Business Profile, which is the long pole on the demand
side, moves one step closer to entering its verification queue.
