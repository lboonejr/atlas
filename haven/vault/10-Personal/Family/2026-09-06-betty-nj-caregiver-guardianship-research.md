## What NJ caregiver/guardianship generally involves (informational research only)

This is general orientation, not legal advice — anything that actually needs filing
should go through an elder-law attorney.

- **Power of Attorney (POA)** is usually the lighter-weight, faster option if Betty
  still has capacity to sign: a durable POA lets grandpa act on her financial/legal
  affairs (bills, banking) without court involvement, and it stays valid even if she
  later loses capacity.
- **Full guardianship** (needed only if she can no longer make her own decisions) is a
  Superior Court / County Surrogate process: file a verified complaint with the county
  Surrogate's Office where Betty lives, supported by **two doctors' certifications**
  (diagnosis + prognosis) based on recent exams. The court appoints an attorney for
  Betty to investigate and report, may hold a hearing, and a judge decides whether to
  appoint a guardian (full or limited).
- Given financial trouble specifically, a narrower **limited/financial guardianship or
  conservatorship** may fit better than full guardianship if Betty's decision-making
  is otherwise intact — worth asking an attorney to scope which track fits.
- NJ Courts' own self-help guardianship pages (njcourts.gov/self-help/guardianship)
  and the county Surrogate's office (whichever county Betty resides in) are the
  practical starting points; an elder-law attorney should handle the actual filing.
