# 1. THE LLC NAME — Given Word LLC

**Lemar's choice: `Given Word LLC`.** Recorded as his call. — **SUPERSEDED 2026-09-16T18:35, see
the LOCKED section at the end of this note: the decision is `Given Word Notary LLC`.**

## Is it available?

**Almost certainly yes, and it cannot be confirmed until it is filed.**

The name clearance run on 2026-09-16 searched the **NJ business name database** for "Given
Word" and "GivenWord" and got **no results**. That is the same state database an LLC name is
checked against, so the evidence is good. It is also the same trademark-clear, competitor-clear
name from that pass.

**Why "confirmed" only happens at filing:** NJ approves a name when the filing is processed, not
when the search runs. Somebody could file the same name first. The gap is days, not months, so
the practical answer is to file rather than to keep checking.

## THE FINDING THAT CHANGES THE PLAN — the $57 county trip is for sole proprietors, not LLCs

The plan currently has Lemar driving to the Camden County Clerk with $57 and a photo ID to file
a **Trade Name Certificate**. That step exists because the business was going to be him,
personally, under a business name.

**A county clerk in NJ registers trade names for individuals and general partnerships only.**
LLCs and corporations do not file there — they register with the **Division of Revenue and
Enterprise Services (DORES)** in Trenton, which is where the LLC itself is formed.

**So forming the LLC replaces the county trip.** The $57, the in-person appearance, the "all
registrants must appear to sign," the backup-names-at-the-counter plan — none of it applies to
an LLC. **Confirm this at the counter or by phone before cancelling anything**, because it
undoes a step that was already researched and scheduled, and a five-minute call is cheap next to
being wrong about it.

## The catch: the entity name and the brand name are not the same thing

The brand is **Given Word Notary**. The entity would be **Given Word LLC**. Those are two
different names, and NJ wants the second one registered before it is used in business.

To trade as "Given Word Notary" under "Given Word LLC," the LLC files a **Registration of
Alternate Name** with DORES: **$50, valid five years, renewable.** Using the name before
registering it costs the $50 plus **a $50 penalty for every year it was used**, and a part year
counts as a whole one.

This also reaches the front end: **the Google Business Profile name has to match the registered
name.** That is why it matters now rather than later.

### Two ways to do it

**Option A — `Given Word Notary LLC`, one filing.**
Entity and brand are the same string. No alternate name, no $50, no five-year renewal to
remember, nothing to keep in sync. The W-9 reads "Given Word Notary LLC," which tells a signing
service exactly what it is looking at. **Cost: $125.**
Downside: the word "Notary" is baked into the legal name. Adding apostille work, fingerprinting,
courier, or anything else later means the name is narrower than the business.

**Option B — `Given Word LLC` plus an alternate name, which is what Lemar picked.**
The legal entity stays broad and can hold other things later; the notary business trades as
"Given Word Notary" underneath it. **Cost: $125 + $50 = $175**, plus a renewal every five years
and one more name to keep straight across the W-9, the Business Profile, the invoices and the
bank account.

**Recommendation: B is a perfectly sound choice** and the extra cost is $50, so this is a
preference, not a mistake. It is the right call if Lemar expects the LLC to hold more than
notary work. A is the right call if he wants the fewest moving parts. **His pick stands unless
he says otherwise.**

### What the W-9 says either way
The W-9 carries the **legal entity name** — `Given Word LLC` — with the trade name on the
business-name line. Every signing service gets that same W-9, which is why the entity has to
exist before platform registration starts.

## Cost and sequence

| Step | Where | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Form the LLC | DORES, online | ~$125 |
| Register the alternate name "Given Word Notary" | DORES | $50 |
| Annual report | DORES, every year | $75/yr |
| ~~Trade Name Certificate~~ | ~~Camden County Clerk~~ | ~~$57~~ — not for an LLC |
| EIN | IRS, online | free |

**Get the EIN.** It is free, it takes minutes, and it means the W-9 going to a dozen signing
services carries an EIN instead of Lemar's Social Security number. That alone is worth the
filing.

**The liability point has not changed and should not be oversold:** NJ holds the individual
notary personally liable for notarial misconduct whatever the entity is. The LLC does not cover
that — **the E&O policy does**. What the LLC buys is B2B credibility and a clean contracting
entity, which is exactly what the signing service channel needs.

Not legal or tax advice. The entity and the EIN are worth a short call with a CPA.

# 2. THE PRINTER — Brother HL-L5210DWT

**Recommendation: `Brother HL-L5210DWT`, roughly $300–350.**

It is the default among working signing agents, and the reason is boring and correct: two paper
trays, so **letter and legal are both loaded at once** and a 150-page loan package prints in one
pass without anyone standing there swapping paper. 48 pages a minute, wired and wireless.

**The HL-L6210DWT (≈$330–400) is the same idea one size up** — heavier duty, bigger toner, lower
cost per page over the long run. It is the right buy for someone printing packages every day. At
the volume Lemar is starting with, it is a more expensive answer to a question he does not have
yet. **Buy the L5210DWT; the L6210DWT is what to buy if the volume arrives.**

## The three things that actually matter

1. **Two trays, both loaded.** One letter, one legal. This is the entire point and it is what
   separates a signing agent's printer from a household one. **Confirm the tray sizes on the
   exact listing before buying** — Brother sells near-identical model numbers where the "T"
   second tray differs, and reviews of these models disagree about the capacities.
2. **Laser, never inkjet.** Inkjet is not a preference here: **county recorders can refuse to
   record a document printed on inkjet**, because the ink runs. A rejected recording on a loan
   package is the expensive kind of mistake.
3. **High-yield toner from day one.** The standard cartridge is the trap. High-yield and
   ultra-high-yield cartridges in this family run 8,000 to 20,000 pages and bring the cost per
   page down to roughly 1.5–3.5¢. At 300 pages per loan package, a cheap cartridge is gone in
   about twenty jobs.

## What it means for the books
Record the printer as a **dated business asset** when it is bought — it is deductible, and nobody
remembers a purchase date two years later. `notary-journal-mirror` already expects this. Toner
and paper receipts replace the estimated printing cost on the jobs they cover.

**Also buy:** a ream of legal and a ream of letter, a spare high-yield toner before it is needed,
and a scanner with a sheet feeder if the printer is not an all-in-one — **scan-backs are due
within hours** and scanning 150 pages on a flatbed is not a plan.

# 3. SIGNING SERVICE PLATFORMS — where to register, in order

The honest headline: **Snapdocs first, and it is not close.** It carries the highest order volume
in most US markets, it is free to join, and a wide range of signing services, title companies and
lenders schedule through it. Registering there is how one profile reaches many companies.

## The order

**1. Snapdocs — first, free, the widest reach.**

Crucially, **"Snapdocs Verified" is the status that gets work**, and it requires exactly five
things:

| Snapdocs Verified needs | Status |
|---|---|
| Notary commission | blocked on the exam |
| Bond, if applicable | with the commission |
| **Full background check report** | the screening queue |
| E&O insurance policy | planned, $100,000 |
| Verified ID | trivial |

**That list is the vendor packet, item for item.** Building the packet is not busywork ahead of
platform registration — it *is* the platform registration.

Two specifics worth getting right the first time:

- **Snapdocs wants the full background check report, not the NNA certificate.** A screenshot of
  an NNA profile is not accepted; companies want the detail. So keep the actual report PDF, not
  just proof that a check happened.
- **The name, email and phone on the NNA profile must match the Snapdocs profile exactly.**
  Mismatches are a known cause of verification not completing. Another reason the entity name
  and contact details should be settled before any of this is typed in.

**NNA certification is not strictly required by Snapdocs** — it runs a nightly check with the NNA
and records whether the course and exam are done. But many hiring companies filter for certified
and screened agents, so the practical answer is still to get certified.

**2. The signing services and title companies that schedule work directly.** Names that come up
repeatedly: **Amrock**, **ServiceLink**, **Coast2Coast Signings**, **Signature Closers**,
**Mortgage Connect**, **Rocket Close**. Several of them also source through Snapdocs, so
registering on Snapdocs does not replace signing up directly — it overlaps. Register with several;
none of them owes anyone volume.

**3. Directories, second tier.** 123notary and NotaryRotary list agents and are where some smaller
companies look. Cheap or free, worth doing after the packet exists, not before.

## What the money actually looks like

- **Most Snapdocs-sourced signings land $75–$150.** Title companies posting directly pay more,
  often **$125–$200+**. That matches the $75 floor already written into `notary-intake`.
- **Net-30 is the normal term**, and the good companies hit it. That is exactly why the unpaid
  alarm was changed off its one-week rule.
- **Some platforms are genuinely bad**, pushing $30 signings and rejecting work over trivial
  errors. Low fee plus slow payment plus a picky reviewer is a job that costs money to do.

## How to vet one before the first job

`notary-intake` already cards a first job from an unknown company rather than booking it. What to
check when that card comes up:

1. **Search the company name on the notary forums** — NotaryCafe and 123notary's forums are where
   non-payment gets reported, usually long before anything official does.
2. **Ask the payment terms in writing** before accepting, and put them on the job record. A
   company that will not state terms is telling you something.
3. **Keep the first job small.** A $75 refi is a cheap way to learn whether they pay.
4. **Two late payments against their own stated terms is a pattern**, not bad luck. The mirror
   surfaces that in the alarm card by design.

## What is still not known
No platform has been registered with, no title company relationship exists, and none of this can
start before the commission and the packet exist. **This note narrows the unknown from "which
platforms?" to "Snapdocs first, then direct registrations, once the packet is built."**
