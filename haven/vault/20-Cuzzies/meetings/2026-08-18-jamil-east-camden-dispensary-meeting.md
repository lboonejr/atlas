## Update 2026-08-15T15:45-04:00 — Jamil meeting prep brief

**Bottom line:** two very different asks could live under "Jamil wants to meet."
Scenario A (license takeover) is a regulated ownership-transfer transaction gated by
NJ CRC and interacts directly with the Off Button wind-down. Scenario B (consulting) is
a services engagement that triggers no CRC filing on its own, but carries its own

### Scenario A — Buy/takeover of the Cuzzie's (Camden) license

NJ CRC treats any meaningful sale/transfer of a licensed dispensary's ownership as a
regulated event, not a private handshake:
- **CRC approval required.** Transfer of any interest of 5%+ in a medical or adult-use
  cannabis dispensary license is subject to Commission approval, conditioned on the
  incoming party completing a criminal history background check. (General framework
  under N.J.A.C. 17:30 — verify current citation/text with counsel.)
- **Lock-up window.** From application submission through at least 2 years after the
  business commences operations, a license holder generally cannot change more than
  50% of its ownership interest in one transaction (narrow carve-outs for a deceased
  owner's heir or joint-licensed spouse/partner). Need to confirm where Cuzzie's
  currently sits relative to that window given the temporary closure — this materially
  affects whether a "sell the whole thing" conversation is even structurally available
  right now.
- **Municipal layer.** Original licensing required a Camden municipal resolution of
  support, zoning approval, and a signed lease. Whether a change-of-ownership requires
  fresh municipal sign-off (vs. notice only) is unclear from public sources — flag for
  counsel, don't assume either way.
- **Not instant.** Published CRC meeting resolutions on ownership transfers (recurring
  through 2025) show these running through full Commission review and a public
  resolution — realistically weeks to months, not a same-day close.

**Interaction with Off Button wind-down:** Cuzzie's is mid vendor wind-down/payoff,
tracked in `haven/vault/40-Projects/on-button-reopen/index.md` and Monday board
`18424191974` ("Off Button — Vendor Wind-Down & Payoff"). Any buyer conversation has to
be sequenced against that — outstanding vendor payables, the tiered payoff plan, and
the license's current operational status (temporarily closed, not surrendered) all
shape what's actually being sold and what a buyer would inherit. A takeover offer is
effectively an offer on the license *plus* whatever liabilities transfer with it —
worth having a current vendor-payoff total in hand before any number gets discussed
with Jamil.

### Scenario B — Consulting/advisory to help Jamil open his own East Camden dispensary

Lemar simply advising Jamil doesn't itself trigger a CRC filing — this would be a
private services arrangement, not a licensing transaction. Rough shape it could take:
- **Scope:** application strategy (most likely a Class 5 retailer license), site/zoning
  review for an East Camden location, help lining up the municipal resolution, and
  operational build-out guidance — Lemar's real value-add being two working NJ
  dispensary operations' worth of first-hand experience.
- **Structure options:** flat consulting fee, hourly/retainer, or (higher upside, higher
  entanglement) a small ownership/equity stake or success fee tied to Jamil's license
  being approved. NJ recently expanded multi-license ownership rights for
  diversely-owned Class 5 retailers (reported 2026 legislative change allows a
  person/entity to hold interest in up to 7 diversely-owned retail licenses, vs. the
  older one-license-per-owner posture) — if Jamil's venture would qualify as
  diversely-owned, an equity stake for Lemar could be more structurally viable than it
  would've been previously. Needs verification against Jamil's actual ownership
  makeup and current CRC rule text.
- **Conflict-of-interest flags:**
  - Cuzzie's own license is in wind-down/temporary closure — advising a new entrant
    while Cuzzie's own house isn't settled could raise fair questions (from Jamil, from
    the CRC, from Cuzzie's own stakeholders) about capacity, bandwidth, and motive.
    Worth being upfront about Cuzzie's status if this is the path taken.
  - If Lemar or Cuzzie's principals take any ownership interest in Jamil's license,
    that becomes a disclosable relationship under CRC ownership-interest rules, and
    could intersect with the same ownership-cap questions relevant to Scenario A.
  - If Scenario A and B blend (e.g., Jamil ends up with an interest in Cuzzie's license
    *and* Lemar advises or holds interest in his), that's a two-way related-party
    situation the CRC would want fully disclosed on both sides — better to flag for
    counsel early than let it surface as an issue later.
